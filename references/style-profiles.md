# Style Profiles

Distilled from the live Arabic sites of the reference brands (observed October 2026). These describe *patterns* to imitate, not text to copy. Where the reference brands themselves are sloppy (Latin leakage, `قم بـ`, inconsistent spelling), this guide says so — imitate their strengths, not their bugs.

## Contents
1. super-app — Careem, Talabat, Noon, Tamara, Tabby

## 1. super-app

**Voice**: light MSA — modern, short, energetic, never stiff. One benefit per sentence, 5–12 words, almost no subordinate clauses. Feels like a confident friend, not an institution.

**Addressing**: masculine singular throughout — imperatives (`اطلب`، `احصل`، `حمّل`، `ادفع`) and the `ـك` possessive (`طلباتك`، `مدفوعاتك`، `رحلاتك`، `أموالك`). No `يمكنك` hedging in marketing; just say it.

**Signature patterns**:
- **One verb, templated across a grid.** Talabat uses `اطلب` + noun for every service tile (`اطلب الطعام`، `اطلب البقالة`). Pick the product's core verb and reuse it — consistency reads as polish.
- **Possessive navigation.** Careem's sections are "your X" / "our X" (`رحلاتك`، `خدماتنا`).
- **Imperative + object** for CTAs and section headings; **imperative + benefit** for heroes (verb first, payoff second).
- **Stacked imperatives** in taglines, comma-separated, ending with `و` (`أنفق، أرسل، ادّخر وأدِر أموالك`) — Tabby.
- **Bare benefit noun phrases** for feature cards (`خطط دفع مرنة`، `حماية المشتري`).
- **Negation as benefit**: `بدون` + noun (`بدون رسوم تأخير`، `بدون فوائد`).
- **Your money/time + verb**: possessive subject then present tense ("your money now works for you" shape).
- **FAQ in user's first person**: `كيف أسجّل؟`، `متى أدفع؟` (Tamara) — the question is the user's voice, the answer is the brand's.
- Selective shadda/kasra on imperatives to prevent misreading: `حمّل`، `فعِّل`، `تحكّم`، `حوّل` (Tabby).

**Loanwords** — three tiers:
1. **Transliterate** consumer-facing feature names and well-known loanwords: `كاش باك`، `أونلاين`، `كباتن` (Careem captains). Only when the brand has already done so.
2. **Keep Latin** for third-party platforms and hardware: `Apple Pay`، `App Store`، `Google Play`، `iPhone`, and sub-brand marks styled in Latin (`Careem Plus`).
3. **Translate** generic nouns: `تطبيق` (never `أبلكيشن`), `محفظة`، `بطاقة`.
Avoid the leakage seen in the wild (untranslated "PAY LATER" banners, `UAE` mid-sentence) — that's a bug.

**Dialect**: base is MSA. Tamara deliberately uses Saudi dialect in FAQ/support voice (`وش بيصير إذا…`، `بس خلّك منتبه`) while keeping hero copy MSA — a good model for `dialect: gulf`.

**Formatting**: Western digits; currency after the number (`19 د.إ شهريًا`، `300 درهم`); percent after digit (`30%`); emoji essentially absent from UI and hero copy.

**Per-brand flavor**:
- *Careem*: everyday-life super-app; possessive taxonomy; Latin sub-brands.
- *Talabat*: relentless `اطلب` discipline; food/grocery warmth.
- *Noon*: (not fetched — general knowledge) bold retail/deal language, short promo labels (`عروض`، `توصيل مجاني`، `الأكثر مبيعًا`).
- *Tamara*: friendly Saudi fintech; MSA marketing + dialect FAQ; first-person questions.
- *Tabby*: crisp financial MSA with diacritics, dense multi-imperative taglines, footnote markers for regulatory disclosures.

## 2. big-tech

**Voice**: clean, precise, formal MSA with zero dialect. Calm and helpful; body copy explains rather than hypes. Support content: neutral, impersonal, 10–20 word sentences.

**Addressing**: masculine singular with `ـك` (`حسابك`، `لديك`). Company speaks as "we" (`كيف يمكننا مساعدتك؟`).

**Three sub-styles — choose one per product**:
- **Apple-like** (devices, premium consumer, design-led): most imperative (`اعرف`، `اشترِ`، `قارن`، `تسوّق`), most literary. Very short verbless headlines, often 2–5 words, with a deliberate full stop as rhythm. Paired adjectives joined by `و`. Light parallelism. Polite `تفضّل بزيارة` in retail. Feature names in straight quotes.
- **Google-like** (SaaS, tools, settings-heavy apps — the safest default for dashboards): **verbal-noun labels** for all UI chrome (`إرسال`، `إغلاق`، `اختيار`، `تخطٍّ`), `يمكنك` in body copy, masdar-headed feature headings (`إدارة الفرق`، `تحديد المواعيد`). The most gender-neutral of the three. Glosses acronyms once: `الذكاء الاصطناعي (AI)`. Reassurance via possessive: `بياناتك في أمان`.
- **Microsoft-like** (enterprise, B2B): plainest and most verb-driven (`استكشف`، `انضم`، `احصل على`). Note: Microsoft frequently uses `قم بـ` + masdar (`قم بتنزيل`) — **don't** imitate this; it's the classic translation smell.

**Learn more** — the three differ (`اعرف المزيد` Apple, `مزيد من المعلومات` Google, `معرفة المزيد` MS). Pick one per product; `اعرف المزيد` is the most natural CTA.

**Terms**: brand/product names always Latin (`iPhone`، `Wi‑Fi`، `USB‑C`، `Gmail`، `Copilot`). Generic tech nouns translated: `تطبيق`، `التخزين السحابي`، `التقويم`، `جهاز لوحي`، `الذكاء الاصطناعي`.

**Punctuation**: `،` and `؟`; no `؛` in practice; exclamation marks essentially never; straight double quotes around embedded names; tashkeel only to disambiguate.

**Formatting**: Western digits; prices often currency-first in Microsoft SA (`ر.س.‏ 42.99/الشهر`) — billing period as `/الشهر`، `/سنة` or `شهريًا`. Dates: `أغسطس 2026`.

## 3. gov-fintech

**Voice**: formal, exact, reassuring MSA. No dialect, no emoji, no exclamation marks in functional UI. Short body sentences (6–14 words); labels 1–4 words.

**Addressing**:
- Actions: masculine singular imperative + `ـك` (`افتح حسابك الآن`، `اطلب بطاقتك`، `تتبّع حالة طلبك`) — Al Rajhi is the cleanest model.
- Greetings and institutional statements: polite plural (`أهلًا بكم`، `يمكنكم`).
- Instructions in gov services: `يُرجى` + verbal noun (`يُرجى إدخال بيانات الدخول`).
- The user as a noun: Saudi banks `العملاء` / `عملاؤنا`; UAE government `المتعاملون`.

**Signature patterns**:
- **Imperative + possessive object** for primary actions (`افتح حسابك`، `اطلب تمويلك`، `حظر بطاقتك`).
- **Verbal-noun navigation** (`إدارة التحويلات`، `التحويل بين الحسابات`، `استرجاع اسم المستخدم`، `إعادة تعيين كلمة المرور`).
- **Audience doorways**: `أفراد` / `أعمال` / `حكومة` (Absher), `الأفراد` / `الشركات`.
- **Fraud & security**: "together" framing + alertness imperative + reporting imperative (`ابقَ يقظًا وبلّغ عن أي نشاط مشبوه`). OTP warnings use the negative imperative: `لا تشارك رمز التحقق مع أي شخص`. Banks never ask for it — say so: `لن نطلب منك رمز التحقق أبدًا`.
- **Compliance surfaced, not hidden**: regulator named (`البنك المركزي السعودي`، `مصرف الإمارات العربية المتحدة المركزي`), complaints path labeled (`تقديم شكوى`), fees/KFS linked prominently.
- Al Rajhi prefers bare imperatives; Emirates NBD uses `قم بـ` — follow Al Rajhi.

**Terms**: acronyms Latin with Arabic gloss on first mention (`تعرّف على عميلك (KYC)`); IBAN → `رقم الآيبان` / `رقم الحساب الدولي (IBAN)`; mada → `مدى`; cookies → `ملفات تعريف الارتباط` (not `كوكيز`); OTP → `رمز التحقق`; product brands (UAE PASS، Visa، Mastercard) Latin.

**Formatting**: Western digits; thousands separator `,`; currency often spelled out after the amount in formal text (`10,000 ريال سعودي`, `10,000 درهم`) and abbreviated in compact UI (`ر.س`، `د.إ`). Dates `13 أكتوبر 2026`; Hijri only in legal/regulatory text (`1448/04/22 هـ`) or where the service is Hijri-based (Saudi government IDs, iqama expiry). Phone numbers LTR.

**Per-site flavor**:
- *Absher*: ministerial voice; plural greeting; short `دخول` for login.
- *Al Rajhi*: crispest imperatives, near-zero Latin, Arabized names — best model for Saudi banking.
- *Emirates NBD*: Latin-brand heavy (`ENBD X`), partnership framing on fraud.
- *u.ae*: `المتعاملين`, accessibility prominent; uses `الرئيسة` for Home (correct MSA; `الرئيسية` is more common in apps).

## 4. egyptian

**Voice**: warm, witty, conversational. A clear **two-tier system**: Egyptian colloquial for heroes, taglines, CTAs, app-download prompts and value props; MSA for nav labels, legal/footer, fees and limits, errors, payment confirmations. This split is the single most important rule of the profile — see `dialects.md`.

**Addressing**: informal masculine singular `انت` implied (`نزّل`، `اطلب`، `انسَ`، `جرّب`). No `انتي`, no gender forking. Plural `-كم` for B2B/merchants/investors (`انضموا لشبكة التجار`). Brand speaks as "we" doing the work for you (`نوصّلك`، `خلّي الأكل علينا`).

**Signature patterns**:
- **Imperative + object + `دلوقتي`** — urgency adverb last (`نزّل التطبيق واطلب دلوقتي`).
- **Transfer of burden**: "forget X, leave Y to us" (`انسَ الطبخ وخلّي الأكل علينا`).
- **Metaphor-as-compression**: category noun in an everyday container ("supermarket in your pocket" → `سوبرماركت في جيبك`).
- **Exhaustive promise**: `كل اللي تحتاجه` + a category list.
- **Two-word benefit stacks** for feature titles (`توصيل سريع`، `خضار طازة`).
- **Universality triplet**: "to anyone, anytime, anywhere" (InstaPay).
- **Brand-name wordplay** in taglines (Valu: `كل لحظة ليها ڤاليو`) — find an Arabic pun, don't translate an English one.
- Humor is light and idiomatic (`من غير وجع دماغ`), never sarcastic; one `!` per section at most.

**Loanwords**: Egyptian brands transliterate more freely than Gulf ones — `أوردر`، `كاش باك`، `باقة`، `ميجا`، `رصيد`، `بيزنس` are natural in dialect copy. Use `ڤ` for /v/ in transliterated brand names when the brand does (`ڤاليو`). Keep `تطبيق` in MSA surfaces; `أبلكيشن` only in very casual dialect copy, and never both on the same page (Valu's inconsistency is a bug). Product names usually stay Latin (`InstaPay`).

**Formatting**: Western digits; amount then unit: `250 جنيه` in prose, `250 ج.م` in compact UI; `قرش` for piasters. Phone numbers `010 1234 5678` / `+20 10 1234 5678`, LTR.

**Per-brand flavor**:
- *Breadfast*: warmest and most colloquial; food metaphors; exhaustive category lists.
- *valU*: dialect-forward, playful, brand puns; (inconsistent spelling — don't copy that).
- *InstaPay*: conservative MSA, fee/limit tables, security vocabulary; dialect only as a light `تقدر تحوّل…` nudge. Model for Egyptian fintech.
- *Talabat Egypt*: pan-Arab MSA, no Egyptian flavor — shows that MSA also works in Egypt for neutral utility copy.
- *Vodafone Egypt* (general knowledge): heaviest dialect in campaigns/push, quantity-forward offers (`ميجا`، `دقايق`), MSA for billing/terms.
- *Fawry* (general knowledge): service-directory MSA, verbal-noun labels (`دفع الفواتير`، `شحن المحفظة`).

## 5. editorial — Thmanyah (ثمانية)

Saudi media house (articles, newsletters, podcasts, subscriptions) with a published style constitution («الدستور» / سياسة النشر). The best model for **blogs, newsletters, long-form landing pages, about/careers pages, podcast/video descriptions, and content-led products**. Use it in place of the other profiles for prose; keep the category profile for the product's functional UI.

**Voice**: modern plain MSA — their rule is fuṣḥā free of complication and pedantry, avoiding hype, preferring sober, clear words. Analytical-essayistic rhythm: claim → evidence → a rhetorical question mid-paragraph (`لكن السؤال هنا: …`). Medium sentences (15–30 words); paragraphs kept short (~100–150 words), ideas broken into bullets. Confident institutional `نحن` (`نؤمن`، `نعتقد`، `نحلل`، `نتوقف عند`) — corporate and editorial voice are the same voice. First-person `I` only in personal essays.

**House bans** (they align with this skill's smells list — apply them strictly in this profile): no `قام بـ`, no `تم` + verbal noun (use a real verb or a true passive: `حُسم`، `ستُحذف`), no `هو/هي` as a copula, no hype or clichés, no italics.

**Dialect**: classical-rooted colloquial words are allowed when the context fits (`أيش` over `ماذا`). Full Saudi/Najdi dialect only in casual teasers for casual shows (`وش`، `يخلّيك`، `تبي`، `لين`، `سولف`). Quoted colloquial asides go in `«»`.

**Addressing**: masculine singular as the neutral default (`تابع بأسلوبك`، `اختر باقتك`، `اشترك`); conversational asides even in UI (`لو تحبّ تسمعه`).

**Headlines** (their rule: a title should carry a question, spark curiosity, or compare):
- `«Work title»` + colon + verdict
- Name + colon + claim
- `كيف / لماذا / ماذا` question
- Two beats joined by `و`
- Result + question tag (`…، كيف حُسم الكلاسيكو؟`)
- Numeral-led titles
- Secondary items as a dek: `زائد: …`; special issues `عدد خاص: …`

**Newsletter / episode teaser formula**: `في العدد الجديد من «X»، نبدأ بـ… ثم ننتقل إلى… قبل أن نتوقف عند…`.

**Loanwords** — a codified system worth following for any Saudi-facing editorial content:
- `ق` for /g/: `قوقل`، `إنقليزي`، `برقر`.
- Drop unneeded long vowels: `فلم`، `أوربا`.
- Naturalized tech words written bare, no quotes: `بودكاست`، `آيفون`.
- Prefer Arabic over transliteration when one exists: `البث` not `ستريمنق`، `مدونة` not `بلوق`، `نشرة بريدية` for newsletter.
- Brands as the company writes itself in Arabic (`أبل`), otherwise Latin.
- Foreign work titles: Arabic in `«»` + original in `()`.
- Latin for tech specs (`4K`، `HDR`) and bilingual job titles (`مدير منتج أول | Senior Product Manager`).

**Formatting (house conventions — use when imitating Thmanyah closely; otherwise follow formatting.md defaults)**:
- Western digits only; Gregorian dates `14 أكتوبر 2026`; durations `1س 42د`.
- `٪` before the number (`٪100`); currency names spelled out in prose (`5,000 ريال`), `ر.س` only in product UI.
- `«»` for titles, brands, terms — even UI words (`«قريبًا»`); `()` for original-language terms.
- `؛` used generously for causal clauses.
- Selective diacritics: shadda + a disambiguating vowel (`حُسم`، `مميّزة`); tanween on the letter, not the alif (`كاتبًا`).
- **Kashida in short labels** (`تابــع`، `سنـــوي`، `المميـــزة`) is a Thmanyah visual signature for optical balance in their own typeface. Don't copy it unless the project explicitly wants the Thmanyah look; it breaks search, screen readers, and other fonts.

**UI labels observed**: `الرئيسية`، `المقالات`، `الإذاعة` (podcasts tab)، `البرامج`، `البحث`، `اشتراك`، `الدخول`، `تابع`، `الأحدث`، `شارك`، `اشترك الآن`، `قارن الباقات`، `للمشتركين فقط`، `الحلقات الجديدة`، `اختر باقتك`، `سنوي / شهري`، `وفّر شهرين`، `قريبًا`، `الأسئلة الشائعة`، `اقرأ المزيد`، `تفعيل القسائم` (redeem vouchers)، `الوظائف الشاغرة`.

**Recurring vocabulary**: `نشرة`، `عدد خاص`، `أبرز`، `نتوقف عند`، `ننتقل إلى`، `حكاية`، `تجربة`، `رصين`، `شغف`، `الوصول إلى المعرفة`.

**Typography**: IBM Plex Sans Arabic; no italics anywhere — guillemets do the work.

## 6. Shared conventions (all reference brands agree)

- **Western digits 0–9** in UI everywhere. Eastern digits only appear sporadically in news/editorial — never mix.
- **Arabic comma `،` and question mark `؟`**; Latin period and `!`.
- **Masculine singular** is the default address in UI across all four groups; plural is for institutional greetings.
- **Brand/product names stay in Latin** unless the brand itself has an official Arabic name (`تمارا`، `طلبات`، `مدى`، `أبشر`).
- **`تطبيق`** for app, **`تسجيل الدخول`** for sign in, **`إنشاء حساب`** for sign up, **`سياسة الخصوصية`**, **`الشروط والأحكام`**, **`الأسئلة الشائعة`**, **`تواصل معنا`/`اتصل بنا`** — near-universal.
- Footers: `من نحن`، `الوظائف`، `خريطة الموقع`، `تابعنا`.
